## Microcontroller/Module Startup Tip

Each module had its own issues where they seemed daunting or just a lot of work. Most of the work is not just academic busy work, but they all build on each other in some shape or form. Looking at things in that mindset helped me get through some of the less exciting modules. I would recommend reading all the instructions fully and then start working on the module. I know that is a lot of back and forth however going through the assignments line by line often leads to mistakes only done by not seeing the full picture. I would also recommend not blindly picking parts that seem fine. Find 3 different major components that are genuinely different but still meet your requirements. That helped picking through all the various options I was looking for control/navigation methods for my subsystem.

## Lessons Learned

1. Double check your button wiring!!
2. Read through your data sheet the whole time, for schematic, pcb and even when soldering. 
3. Build the code in modules and share with teammates if they need to mirror or reflect said modules. 
4. Start modeling your case/housing early (While not a measured requirement of this course, it helps with making the final product look more complete). 
5. Test early, breadboard your whole system as soon as you can if you can. 
6. Have consistent messaging with your teammates, talking two times a week is not enough. 
7. Have a clear plan for team meetings so they do not take 4 hours and only do 1 hour of work. 
8. Be prepared to trade parts and knowledge with your team so you all succeed. 
9. Make sure to maintain a healthy balance between your classes. Never let one class take up so much time and focus where you start hurting in other places. 
10. It's okay to make mistakes, no one masters anything through simply reading instruction, you have to fail to know what you need to do better. 


## Recommendations for Future Students

1. Work hard on this project, there is a lot to learn from it. 
2. Use up those office hours, even if the TAs and the Professor have to leave, work in the lab; someone will help you and maybe you can help someone else
3. Get close with your team, not just in school, but try to meet outside of it as well. 
4. Get familiar with other teams, most people are happy to help, especially for this class. 
5. Rest, you will break things if you push too hard after 5+ hours on a solder bench. 
6. Be patient with the TAs and the Professor, they are trying to help a whole class of first time students do a whole bunch of new skills in a very short amount of time.
